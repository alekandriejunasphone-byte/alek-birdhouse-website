How I Built It

My 20 class steps, tools and safety notes.

Check missing dimensions in the teacher’s guide. Get trained before using machines.

1. Measuring and marking timber

I marked the seven timber pieces.

Tools: Ruler, try square, pencil.

Safety: Confirm dimensions, timber thickness and saw allowance before cutting.

2. Cutting the seven timber pieces

I cut the timber, leaving the base separate.

Tools: Tenon saw, bench hook.

Safety: Secure timber; keep the holding hand behind and clear of the blade.

3. Marking the roof triangle

I marked matching roof triangles.

Tools: Ruler, try square, pencil.

Safety: Use the teacher’s roof angle and triangle height.

4. Cutting the triangle shapes

I cut both triangles to match.

Tools: Teacher-approved saw.

Safety: Support work; fingers clear. Scroll saw: eye protection, hair/clothes secured; stop before clearing waste.

5. Marking the entrance hole

I marked the entrance centre.

Tools: Ruler, pencil, class drawing.

Safety: Confirm entrance diameter and position with the teacher.

6. Marking the perch

I marked the perch below it.

Tools: Ruler, pencil.

Safety: Check spacing against the drawing. Use an 8 mm hole.

7. Drilling the bird entrance and 8 mm perch hole

I drilled the entrance and perch hole.

Tools: Drill press, approved entrance cutter, 8 mm bit, backing board, clamps.

Safety: Teacher training and approval required. Clamp work; remove chuck key. Wear eye protection; secure hair/clothes; hands clear. No gloves. Stop fully before adjusting.

8. Marking Roof 2

I marked Roof 2’s overlap strip.

Tools: Ruler, pencil.

Safety: Confirm strip width with the teacher; it depends on timber thickness.

9. Cutting the roof strip

I cut the strip and checked the fit.

Tools: Tenon saw, bench hook.

Safety: Secure work. Ask the teacher about safely cutting narrow strips.

10. Tracing and cutting the porch

I traced and cut the porch.

Tools: Pencil, class template, approved saw.

Safety: Fingers clear. Stop powered saws before clearing waste.

11. Sanding the timber pieces

I sanded the rough edges.

Tools: Sandpaper, sanding block; disc sander only if approved.

Safety: Eye protection and school dust controls. Disc sander: approved downward-moving side, work on table, fingers clear; no tiny pieces.

12. Marking nail positions

I marked nails and checked for square.

Tools: Pencil, ruler, try square.

Safety: Teacher checks nail size/spacing for timber thickness. Pilot holes if required.

13. Attaching the porch

I lined up and attached the porch.

Tools: Hammer, approved nails, clamps or support.

Safety: Eye protection; support joint; fingers clear of hammer.

14. Nailing together the walls

I joined the walls and checked the base.

Tools: Hammer, nails, try square, clamps.

Safety: Follow the teacher’s base-fixing method. Support work; no protruding nail points.

15. Nail punching and filling with putty

I punched nails and filled the holes.

Tools: Nail punch, hammer, wood putty.

Safety: Eye protection; hold punch steady. Follow putty handling/drying instructions.

16. Measuring roof position

I checked and marked the roof position.

Tools: Ruler, pencil, try square.

Safety: Support loose panels; check overhang against drawing.

17. Attaching the roof panels

I attached both roof panels.

Tools: Hammer, approved nails, clamps or support.

Safety: Support roof; eye protection; fingers clear. Approved nail length; no points inside.

18. Nail punching the roof

I punched the roof nails below the surface.

Tools: Nail punch, hammer.

Safety: Eye protection; support box. Check splits, loose joints and sharp nail points.

19. Final sanding using 80, 120 and 240 grit

I sanded along the grain: 80, 120, then 240 grit.

Tools: 80, 120 and 240 grit sandpaper, sanding block.

Safety: Use required dust extraction/protection. Check nail points; never blow dust.

20. Attaching bark to the roof using hot glue

I glued fallen bark onto the roof.

Tools: Hot glue gun, glue sticks, fallen bark.

Safety: Teacher supervision; heat-resistant mat and stand. Avoid hot nozzle/glue. Unplug; let cool. Collect fallen bark with permission, never from living trees.

Tools and final checks

I follow the teacher’s tool instructions.

Before taking it home, check splinters, joints, nails and the bark/glue’s outdoor durability.
