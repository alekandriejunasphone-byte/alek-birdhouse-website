How I Built It

These are the 20 steps from the sequence in my project brief. Each step has a short explanation, the tools and the main safety points.

The teacher’s original handout was not supplied. Check it for missing dimensions and follow the teacher’s training before using any machine.

1. Measuring and marking timber

I marked the seven pieces on the 1200 mm timber using the class cutting layout.

Tools: Ruler, try square, pencil.

Safety: Check the dimensions, timber thickness and saw allowances with the teacher before cutting.

2. Cutting the seven timber pieces

I cut the seven timber pieces on the waste side of the marks. The plywood base was a separate piece.

Tools: Tenon saw, bench hook.

Safety: Support the timber securely and keep the holding hand behind and clear of the blade.

3. Marking the roof triangle

I marked matching roof triangles on the 140 × 200 mm front and back blanks.

Tools: Ruler, try square, pencil.

Safety: Use the roof angle and triangle height in the teacher’s drawing; they were not supplied in the brief.

4. Cutting the triangle shapes

I cut the triangle shapes and checked that the front and back matched.

Tools: Teacher-approved saw.

Safety: Support the work and keep fingers clear. On a scroll saw, secure hair and loose clothing, wear eye protection and stop before clearing waste.

5. Marking the entrance hole

I marked the entrance centre on the front panel.

Tools: Ruler, pencil, class drawing.

Safety: Confirm the entrance diameter and position with the teacher before drilling.

6. Marking the perch

I marked the perch hole below the entrance.

Tools: Ruler, pencil.

Safety: Check its position against the class drawing. The hole is 8 mm; do not guess the spacing.

7. Drilling the bird entrance and 8 mm perch hole

I drilled the entrance and the 8 mm perch hole in the front panel.

Tools: Drill press, approved entrance cutter, 8 mm bit, backing board, clamps.

Safety: Use only after training and teacher approval. Clamp the work, remove the chuck key, wear eye protection, secure hair and clothing, and keep hands clear. No gloves near rotating parts; wait for a full stop before adjustments.

8. Marking Roof 2

I marked the strip to remove from Roof 2 so the roof panels could overlap.

Tools: Ruler, pencil.

Safety: Confirm the strip width with the teacher; it depends on the timber thickness and class design.

9. Cutting the roof strip

I cut the marked strip from Roof 2 and checked the fit with Roof 1.

Tools: Tenon saw, bench hook.

Safety: Support the piece securely. Ask the teacher for a safe cutting method if the strip is too narrow to hold safely.

10. Tracing and cutting the porch

I traced the porch shape onto the 90 × 140 mm blank and cut it out.

Tools: Pencil, class template, approved saw.

Safety: Keep fingers clear of the cutting line. Stop a powered saw before removing waste.

11. Sanding the timber pieces

I sanded the rough edges before joining the pieces.

Tools: Sandpaper, sanding block; disc sander only if approved.

Safety: Use eye protection and the school’s dust-control rules. On a disc sander use the designated downward-moving side, support work on the table and keep fingers clear. Do not use tiny pieces against the disc.

12. Marking nail positions

I marked the nail positions and checked the joints for square.

Tools: Pencil, ruler, try square.

Safety: Have the teacher check nail size and spacing for the timber thickness. Use pilot holes if required.

13. Attaching the porch

I lined up the porch and attached it using the class fixing method.

Tools: Hammer, approved nails, clamps or support.

Safety: Wear eye protection, support the joint and keep fingers away from the hammer.

14. Nailing together the walls

I joined the walls and checked the fit of the 165 × 165 mm plywood base.

Tools: Hammer, nails, try square, clamps.

Safety: Follow the teacher’s base-fixing method. Support the work and check that nail points do not stick through.

15. Nail punching and filling with putty

I punched the wall nail heads below the surface and filled the holes with putty.

Tools: Nail punch, hammer, wood putty.

Safety: Wear eye protection and hold the punch steadily. Follow the putty instructions for handling and drying.

16. Measuring roof position

I checked how the roof panels sat on the walls and marked their positions.

Tools: Ruler, pencil, try square.

Safety: Support the loose panels and check the overhang against the teacher’s drawing.

17. Attaching the roof panels

I attached the roof panels and checked that they met neatly at the top.

Tools: Hammer, approved nails, clamps or support.

Safety: Support the roof, wear eye protection and keep fingers clear. Use the approved nail length so points do not project inside.

18. Nail punching the roof

I used the nail punch to set the roof nail heads below the surface.

Tools: Nail punch, hammer.

Safety: Wear eye protection and support the box on the bench. Check for splits, loose joints and sharp nail points.

19. Final sanding using 80, 120 and 240 grit

I sanded with 80, then 120, then 240 grit, following the grain.

Tools: 80, 120 and 240 grit sandpaper, sanding block.

Safety: Control dust using the workshop’s required extraction and protection. Check for nail points and do not blow sanding dust into the air.

20. Attaching bark to the roof using hot glue

I attached loose bark to the roof with hot glue for the class finish.

Tools: Hot glue gun, glue sticks, fallen bark.

Safety: Use teacher supervision, a heat-resistant mat and the gun’s stand. Keep away from the hot nozzle and glue, then unplug and let it cool. Collect fallen bark with permission; do not strip living trees.

Tools and final checks

The tools were a try square, ruler, tenon saw, bench hook, drill press, scroll saw, disc sander, hammer, nail punch, sandpaper and hot glue gun. I need to use each one as shown by the teacher.

Before taking the project home, it needs checking for splinters, loose joints and sharp nail points. Bark and hot glue are a class finish, so outdoor durability still needs checking.
